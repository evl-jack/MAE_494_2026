# Shared failure: matched-state gradients and initial-action interventions

This diagnostic deliberately examines development group 603 because both 4C and 3D_Long failed it in the preceding rollout comparison. It is a post-hoc case study, not a new independent test or evidence that a correction generalizes. All four poses are retained. The networks, dynamics, horizon, numerical solver and fine-reference pursuer feedback remain unchanged.

Before new traces or interventions, `protocol.json` fixes both models, two control intervals, five prefix durations and two intervention types. The primary intervention holds the reference's initial evader turn for 5 ms, then returns to the learned controller at dt=.005. The other fixed durations are 10, 25, 50 and 100 ms. One family holds the original reference turn constant during the prefix; another recomputes reference feedback during it. A full-reference evader supplies a separate comparison. Prefix interventions are diagnostic reference assistance, not a trained improvement or deployable model capability.

The pursuer uses fine-reference feedback on each actual trajectory at every control update. Its realized actions can change when the evader changes the trajectory; they are not a prerecorded disturbance sequence. This compares closed-loop policies against the same feedback rule, rather than claiming that every arm faces an identical action history or an exact best response.

At both .01- and .005-second control intervals, controls are held through each interval and motion follows the previously checked exact constant-turn arcs. If a prefix ends between nominal updates, its exact endpoint is inserted as an extra event. The primary 5 ms prefix is aligned with the .005-second grid. Nine within-interval arc samples and the relative-speed distance bound distinguish definite from possible contact. Contact flags remain absorbing, though mathematical traces continue to the horizon; post-contact portions of plots are diagnostic continuations.

Coarse and fine references use the same 97×97×72 and 129×129×96 grids as development evaluation, with 21 saved time planes. Regenerated initial values must reproduce the old saved values within 1e-12 m. Values and central-grid derivatives are interpolated in space and linearly between times, as in the prior rollout policy. Coarse/fine coefficient signs and magnitudes are reported, including the previously used 0.005, 0.01 and 0.02 m/rad margins. Their agreement is a resolution-stability diagnostic, not proof of the exact optimal direction.

Both reference resolutions and the learned model are queried at the same states on each actual trajectory. This avoids interpreting differences between two already diverged trajectories as pure gradient error. The traces save time remaining, joint states, learned/coarse/fine values, both actor switching coefficients, actual controls and geometric contact information. The evader coefficient is `V_heading_e` in joint coordinates or `ry*V_rx - rx*V_ry - V_psi` in relative coordinates. The pursuer coefficient is `V_heading_p` or `V_psi`; its minimizing action has the opposite sign.

At the initial state, double-precision copies of the trained networks are checked with central finite differences (epsilon 1e-5) on physical joint headings. For the 3D model the perturbed joint state is reduced before querying it. Agreement verifies the derivative computation of the learned function; it does not establish that the learned derivative is accurate for the game. Original network parameters remain unchanged and accumulate no parameter gradients.

Synthetic fixtures checked value/gradient time interpolation and an exact 25 ms handback event before this diagnostic ran. Baseline minimum separations and contact flags are compared with the preceding saved rollout. This comparison can expose batch-size or controller-rounding sensitivity rather than silently assuming exact reproduction. No optimizer, training, future final-state data, adaptive duration search or production-controller modification is involved.
