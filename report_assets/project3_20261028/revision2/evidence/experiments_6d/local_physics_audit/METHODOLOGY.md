# Local physics-loss visibility audit

This audit holds the trained 4C and 3D_Long models fixed. It uses the existing development failure and saved 161/193-grid reference maps. No new reference solve, rollout, optimizer, parameter step, candidate checkpoint or final-test query is performed. Regions and weightings are fixed in `protocol.json` before querying residuals or loss gradients.

The local map is the previous 21 early states × 201 physical evader-heading offsets, lifted to four equivalent poses. This gives 16,884 joint queries, with correlated poses and neighboring points. The same intrinsic states enter the 3D model after coordinate reduction. Reference-stable states require the 161- and 193-grid evader coefficients to have the same sign and both magnitudes above .01 m/rad. Reference labels distinguish diagnostic right/wrong subsets; they never enter a training update.

Fresh global collocation audits use 32,768 points per model with independent seeds 991760 and 991761. Positions and headings follow each model's own training box, time is uniform over the full .5-second horizon, and the trailing one-eighth of samples have zero time as in training. Because the models use different coordinate representations, their global distributions are not identical physical encounter distributions. Comparisons therefore report within-model global percentiles and a global late-time subset (tau ≥ .4), matching the local time range. These are fresh sampling snapshots, not a reconstruction of which states training visited.

The audit separates `V_tau`, `H`, equation term `V_tau-H`, obstacle term `V-l` and native VI residual `R=max(V_tau-H,V-l)`. The training loss uses mean `abs(R)`. Absolute/squared residual summaries, raw equation errors and active-branch counts are retained. Native residual magnitudes use the existing implementation's PDE/obstacle scaling; they are not value-error distances, probabilities or direct safety bounds. In an obstacle problem the equation term need not vanish where the obstacle is active, so an obstacle-selected residual is not automatically a loss bug.

Residuals are compared with saved value/coefficient error and turning-sign mismatch. Descriptive ROC AUC measures whether larger absolute residual ranks wrong-sign states above right-sign states within the reference-stable set. Quantiles and correlations describe this local, post-hoc sample only; no inferential independence or generalization claim is made. The reference-based instantaneous Hamiltonian shortfall is `3*abs(a_ref) - 3*sign(a_model)*a_ref`; it is a numerical diagnostic, not accumulated real-world cost.

The Hamiltonian uses magnitudes of the control coefficients because its max/min optimization was already carried out analytically. A formal pointwise reflection `a -> -a`, holding its other arguments fixed, leaves `H` unchanged. The code checks this identity. It is not a realizable network update or an alternative globally integrable value function: terminal conditions, derivatives and the PDE's global solution geometry still constrain the sign. This check helps explain why a local scalar residual alone is insufficient to certify a turning decision.

Parameter gradients are computed for mean absolute physics residual under global/all, global/late, uniform/local, reference-wrong/local and reference-right/local subsets. Three additional fixed detached weightings emphasize the network's own small switching coefficient, the central heading window, or the earliest half of the local time interval. Each weighting is normalized over its full dataset before chunked gradient accumulation. There is no differentiation through membership/weights and no optimizer. The wrong/right masks are reference-informed diagnostic subsets, not a proposed reference-free training sampler. Gradient norms and pairwise cosines assess physics-loss priority conflicts; they do not establish that a step would improve reference accuracy or collision outcomes.

A global intrinsic-window count uses rx/ry within .1 m and circular relative heading within .2 rad of the initial failure, at tau ≥ .4. It is only a snapshot of sampling density and cannot prove that training missed the region. The audit does not equate a small count with a causal sampling failure.

Analytic fixtures compare native residuals with the decomposed audit expression, verify joint/relative residual equivalence and check a closed-form Hamiltonian. Original checkpoint hashes and earlier query arrays are protected. All parameter-gradient computations use `autograd.grad` without accumulating `.grad` or updating model weights. Results are saved in the project mirrors and viewed in VS Code; execution uses the managed main-project interpreter.
